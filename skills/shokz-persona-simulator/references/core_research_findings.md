# Shokz Core Research Findings

Source: `2026北美品牌用户调研（测试Agent用） _脱敏进Skill版本.xlsx`. Base: Shokz purchasers, n=633.

Use this reference as evidence when evaluating Shokz product, feature, campaign, slogan, and creative concepts. Cite the relevant finding as supporting evidence, caution evidence, or neutral context. Do not treat these findings as deterministic predictions; combine them with the persona simulation in `personas.md`.

## Executive Takeaways

- The sample is gender-polarized but male-leaning overall: Male 56%; Female 42%; Non-binary person/third gender 1%.
- Average age is 39.5; 31% are 35-44 and 31% are 45+.
- Usage is frequent: Daily 58%; Several times per week 33%; Once per week 5%.
- Core scenarios are active but not only athletic: Running / Jogging 60%; Outdoor activities (e.g., hiking, city walking) 55%; Gym / Fitness 49%; Relaxation / leisure 47%; Commuting 38%; Home tasks (cooking, cleaning, chores) 36%.
- The strongest purchase drivers are practical: Comfort and fit 48%; Sound quality 46%; Product quality 40%; Value for money 35%; Design and style 30%; Battery life 30%; Brand reputation 28%.
- Brand equity is anchored in comfort, quality, sound, open-ear awareness, and active/sport use: Comfortable 41%; High quality 34%; Great sound 30%; Sporty 29%; Durable 29%; Lightweight 28%; Trustworthy 26%; Open-ear 25%.
- Quality and loyalty are strong: quality mean 4.27/5, satisfaction mean 4.27/5, repurchase mean 4.27/5, NPS 42.

## Evidence Use For Concept Evaluation

When evaluating a concept, add an evidence section after the persona table:

- **Support evidence**: cite findings that make the concept more plausible, such as high running usage for sports concepts, high comfort/fit and quality drivers for product-performance claims, or high ambient-awareness purchase motivation for open-ear safety concepts.
- **Caution evidence**: cite findings that create risk, such as low support for a niche scenario, a concept that ignores practical drivers, or messaging that leans only on abstract brand emotion without comfort/quality/use-case proof.
- **Evidence gap**: say when the concept needs information not covered by the survey, such as price elasticity, creator preference, retail conversion, or competitive claim testing.

## Concept Evidence Guide

When evaluating a new concept, use only the statistical findings already summarized in this file. Do not require access to the original Excel workbook.

- Sports/running/gym concepts: use the running, gym/fitness, cycling, outdoor-activity, and exercise-frequency findings in "Usage And Activity".
- Everyday multitasking concepts: use home tasks, individual work/study, work calls/meetings, commuting, ambient-awareness purchase motivation, and multi-scenario purchase motivation.
- Product performance concepts: use comfort/fit, sound quality, product quality, battery life, Bluetooth stability, perceived quality, satisfaction, and repurchase findings.
- Brand image or slogan concepts: use brand association words, perceived advantages, brand personality traits, brand clarity, and brand liking findings.
- Service/warranty concepts: use warranty/customer-service purchase motivation, support findability, and support satisfaction findings.
- If a concept depends on evidence not summarized here, such as price elasticity, creator fit, retail conversion, or competitor-claim testing, state it as an evidence gap rather than inventing support.
